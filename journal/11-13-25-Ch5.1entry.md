## Reflection
This chapter was interesting to read, as it focused on the aspect of error commited by people. It was an eye-opener to read that we tend to blame people for making mistakes when using/doing something yet we don't consider the possibility of asking ourselves that it may be the design's fault that we failed. In the beginning of the chapter, it was interesting to learn about the different methods for finding out why something doesn't work, such as the Root Cause Analysis and The Five Whys.

Throughout the chapter, it was interesting to learn about the different types of errors and how they can be classified into two major ones, those being slips and mistakes, and within those two there different versions.

Out of those errors, one factor that caught my attention the most was social pressure. While it is not an error itself, it greatly contributes to the person's chances of making an error, depending on the context.


## Notes
* Errors commonly happen because of the nature of tasks and procedures that require people to behave in unnatural ways, interruptions, and the attitude people have towards errors (163)
* We tend to punish guilty parties when errors cause financial loss, injuries, or death, blaming them and having them trained so the error won’t happen again, but instead not fix the design of the product (163)
* **Root Cause Analysis**: investigate the accident until a single, underlying cause if found (164)
* **The Five Whys**: when searching for the reason for an error, even if you found the cause, keeping asking why that was the case (165)
* If the system lets you make an error, it is badly designed, if the system induces you to make an error, it is really badly designed (167)
* There are different types of violations that we tend to risk taking such as **routine** and **situational** (168)
* A major cause of violations are rules or procedures that not only invite violation but encourage it (169)
* Human errors seem to be unintentional, two major ones being slips and mistakes (170)
* **Slips** happen when you achieve a goal but executed it wrong, resulting in another goal (171)
* **Mistakes** happen when you are trying to achieve an incorrect goal in an incorrect way (171)
* An issue that causes errors is **social pressure**, where the environment that social pressure takes effect can lead to misinterpretation, mistakes, and accidents (186)
* Good design isn’t enough to overcome social problems, we need different training and instead reward safety and have it as a priority over economic pressure (189)
* **Checklists** are used to increase accuracy of behavior and to reduce the probability of errors occurring, more specifically slips and memory lapses (189)
* They are important on situations involving multiple problems, complex requirements, and interruptions (189)
* They are more efficient when two people are involved in making checklists, having one read instructions and the other executing it (189)
* Creating a checklist with a set order isn’t great because it causes people to execute the checklist in a specific order with a specific expectation of the result when there are times that an item on the checklist can’t be done and can cause confusion (191)
